The decision this document records

A console is operated, not read

The consoles used to carry their own explanation: paragraphs above each panel saying what the panel was for, a six-card walkthrough telling a presenter what to point at. It was accurate and it read as a description of a system rather than a system.

An officer at 07:00 has a queue to clear. A commander at a morning brief has one question. Neither of them is a student of the design. So the explanatory layer was lifted out — into this document — and the screens were rebuilt as dense, scannable, action-first surfaces.

What the screens were

Full-width prose cards. Every panel introduced before it was shown. /demo was six numbered steps of presenter script. A visitor read about what the system would show them.

What the screens are

A filterable queue with a close-out that blocks. A sub-unit matrix with one headline. A tabbed assurance console. /demo is now the control room: live stage timings, funnel counts, four doors.

Nothing was deleted. Everything the screens used to say is on this page, and the presenter script survives intact as its own document.

Plate 4 of 4

Personnel

This console has to be worth opening for the person’s own benefit. If it reads as surveillance, adoption collapses — and a voluntary self-assessment is worth a fifth of the evidence gate on its own, so that channel dying is not a cosmetic loss.

1 Complete, or not offered. A missing translation key is a build failure, never an English fallback — a consent screen that switches to English halfway through has stopped being a consent screen. Script-aware typography: uppercase and letter-spacing are disabled outside Latin.
2 The three things a person needs on opening: what they have allowed, whether a welfare officer may contact them, and whether their consent is on record.
3 Deliberately above the toggles. Knowing what becomes visible is a precondition of agreeing to it, so the order is not negotiable even though it costs space above the actions.
4 Per-domain, and voice has its own toggle. Agreeing to have your leave record read is not agreeing to be recorded, and defaulting it on would make the distinction meaningless. It is off by default.
5 Real PHQ-9 items and real scoring. Item 9 bypasses every gate — it routes straight to a mental-health authority with an SLA measured from submission, without waiting for a persistence window. A validated instrument's translation cannot be invented; the locale files refuse to serve one that has not been supplied.
6 Consent is to a specific text, in a specific language, at a specific version. The receipt records all three. An unreviewed translation cannot ground consent, so saving is refused against draft text rather than accepted quietly.
7 The one card on the page that never folds. Everything else a person reads once on enrolment is behind a disclosure triangle; a person in difficulty should not have to find and click one to reach a helpline number.

The object every plate is arranged around

One case, refused by 0.006

Both the officer’s console and the auditor’s render this same object. It is the reason the system exists: every reason to name somebody, and a refusal.

evidence 1.000 CLEAR
consistency 0.694 HOLD
persistence 0.965 CLEAR
actionability 1.000 CLEAR

consistency = mean(0.667, 0.708, 0.708) = 0.694  ·  needs 0.700

Why this is the moment, and not the escalation

Three domains deviating, sustained across months, evidence at 1.000. On any dashboard built around a risk score this is a flag. But 45–61% of this jawan’s unit is deviating the same way, because the unit is deployed — and a unit-wide pattern is a command workload problem, not an individual welfare concern.

No name is produced. Instead the system states what would change its mind, computed by inverting the gate that failed:

“the duty_roster deviation is still present after the unit’s current deployment cycle ends (op-tempo confounder, 0.70)”

The audit of this change

What moved, and where it went

Screen What it used to carry Where that is now
/officer Introductory prose per panel; gate explanations inline; close-out categories described before use. Plate 1 above. The screen kept the reading order that matters — counter-argument above the buttons.
/unit Paragraphs on k-anonymity, differential privacy and the release cache. Plate 2 above. The screen kept a short “not shown here” list, because a commander should be able to see the limits without leaving.

/demo Six numbered cards of presenter script: what to say, what to point at, why. Its own document — the demo run-sheet. The screen is now the control room.

What deliberately stayed on the screens

  • The counter-argument, above the action buttons
  • “Not shown here” on the commander’s view
  • What each role cannot see, on the control-room doors
  • The helpline card, unfolded, on the personnel app
  • Every refusal message — a refused page explains itself rather than disappearing

The honest limits

  • There is no real dataset. The cohort is generated from seed 26186, and the training label is circular — the model learns the generator.
  • Voice cannot detect somebody who says “I’m fine” and sounds fine. That limit is written down as a test, not hidden.
  • Compute scales to a national force on one server. The escalation rate does not scale — that is a threshold and staffing decision, and the dial is exposed.
